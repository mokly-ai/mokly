## Share pins what you are reading

Share copies the address of the publication you have open, with the screen, the
viewport and the color scheme you selected. A share link always pins that
publication; it never follows the branch to a later one.

Sharing changes nothing about who may open the link. It gives no one access
they did not already have.

## Private and public projects

A project is private until you change it. Share from a private project and the
dialog says **Project members can open this link**. Share from a public one and
it says **Anyone with the link can view**.

Public makes publications readable to anyone holding a link. It grants no
write access, and making the project private again ends that reading on the
next request.

## Opening a link you were sent

Opening a private link while signed out brings you back to that exact link once
you sign in. If you still do not have access, the page says you need access to
the project and offers a way back to your organizations; it tells you nothing
about what is inside. A link to a publication that has been deleted or has
expired says the publication is unavailable.

## Access is checked on every request

Every request checks access again. Removing someone from a project, or turning
a public project private, takes effect the next time they ask for anything.
